# Spatial and cross-scene continuity

## Location world versus state

The world locks topology, doors/windows, fixed facilities, primary anchor, scale, and material baseline. A state may change time, atmosphere, practical lights, movable dressing, or causal damage. Leaving and returning to an unchanged place reuses the same state instead of reinterpreting it.

## Required views

Every scene state has an empty establishing wide and an independently generated `side_three_quarter_wide`, normally 30–60° from the approved front axis. It must expose an adjacent plane, depth, a reachable camera position, and the primary anchor. A crop or slight shift is not a new view.

Multi-axis coverage expands to front, left/right three-quarter, and reverse wide.

## Primary anchor and coordinates

Choose exactly one stationary, distinctive, scale-readable object with production or story causality. Its ground-contact center is `(0,0,0)`; `+X/+Y` receive stable real-world names and `+Z` points up. Coordinates use approximately 0.1 m precision and always include human-readable landmark relations.

## Ready-shot contract

A shot binds the exact spatial map, camera mark, subject marks, set-fixed facing, anchor projection, distances, depth order, and movement start/end marks. Screen left/right may be included, but it can never replace anatomical side or fixed set direction because reverse angles flip screen projection.

Spatial drift, floating anchors, unreachable cameras, missing off-screen distance, path-through-wall motion, or disagreement between map, Photoboard, keyframe, and prompt blocks production.
